Step 1 of 5

Understand what goes into the bottle.

PG · propylene glycol

A thinner carrier. It helps a liquid flow through smaller wicking openings and can give a more noticeable throat sensation.

VG · vegetable glycerine

A thicker carrier. It contributes to vapour production, but a thick mix needs a device that can wick it properly.

The ratio describes the whole finished liquid. This app labels ratios PG/VG: 30/70 means 30% PG and 70% VG. Other sites sometimes write VG first, so always check the labels.

Draw style and nicotine form describe different things.
TermWhat it means
MTLMouth to lung: a tighter draw, into the mouth first and then the lungs.
DTLDirect to lung: a more open draw, usually with higher airflow and power.
Nicotine salts / freebaseForms of nicotine, not PG/VG ratios or draw styles. Salt nicotine does not mean “suitable for every device.” Match the liquid and strength to the device manufacturer's guidance.

Nicotine-free liquid contains no added nicotine. You can practise the calculator and bench without handling nicotine.

Step 2 of 5

Start with what you actually have.

  1. Open the flavour library. Search by flavour and manufacturer, then check the exact version—V1 and V2 can be different flavours.
  2. Open the flavour and choose Add to stash. Enter the quantity you really have in ml, then save it. Add your other concentrates the same way.
  3. Keep labelled PG, VG and any nicotine base beside the recipe setup. The flavour stash is for concentrates; nicotine strength and carrier are entered in the recipe calculator and checked again before mixing.

If you will use nicotine later

Read the bottle label. Nicotine base (mg/ml) is the strength of that stock bottle; Target nicotine strength (mg/ml) is what you want in the finished mix. These are different numbers. Do not guess an unlabelled strength or treat a percentage as mg/ml.

Set Nicotine carrier to PG or VG to match the label. If the base uses a mixed carrier the app cannot represent, stop and get that resolved rather than picking an approximation.

Nicotine-containing liquid can cause poisoning through exposure. Avoid skin/eye contact, follow the product's handling instructions and keep liquids closed and away from children and pets. For an exposure, seek local poison-service or medical advice promptly. The first practice option below is 0 mg/ml.

Step 3 of 5

Make a small recipe you can check.

  1. Use the practice button below to open a private, 10 ml, 0 mg/ml draft. Nothing is saved or mixed just by opening it.
  2. Give it a name and choose flavours you own. Start from the concentrate supplier's guidance or a recipe you trust. Recipe usage averages are observations, not single-flavour testing instructions.
  3. Enter each flavour's percentage. Check Batch size, Target nicotine strength, PG % and VG %. For this nicotine-free practice, keep the target at 0.
  4. Review Estimated final PG/VG and the ingredient amounts before saving. If the recipe cannot reach your chosen ratio, change the inputs rather than ignoring the warning.

Where did the concentrate's PG go?

Arithmetic example only—not a recommended flavour percentage: a 10 ml, 30/70 PG/VG, nicotine-free mix has room for 3 ml PG and 7 ml VG. If the example concentrate contributes 1 ml of PG, you only add another 2 ml of plain PG.

1 ml PG from flavour+ 2 ml plain PG+ 7 ml VG= 10 ml at 30/70 PG/VG

The calculator includes carrier from concentrates and nicotine. Check carrier assumptions where shown; an unknown carrier is not a verified measurement. Percentages are by volume, while the bench's gram targets use ingredient density—do not treat 1 ml as 1 g for every ingredient.

Step 4 of 5

Your recipe becomes a mixing bench.

  1. Save the recipe, open it, and choose Start guided mix. Confirm the actual batch size, nicotine settings and PG/VG targets before starting.
  2. Set out a suitable bottle, ingredients and a working scale. Follow each on-screen tare and pour instruction. The right tray is waiting, the middle is in use, and the left is finished.
  3. If nicotine is included, nicotine is always first. Resolve any nicotine mistake before adding other ingredients. For a 0 mg mix, there is no nicotine pour.
  4. Enter the real scale reading after a pour. If you make a mistake, pause and use the bench's recovery instructions. Do not hide an overpour by entering the target reading or taring during recovery.
  5. Confirm when the ingredient bottle has been moved aside, then continue. The receiving-bottle fill is an estimate from recorded amounts, not a live scale connection.

The walkthrough does not change recovery rules. The live bench tells you what to do for the actual ingredient and reading.

Step 5 of 5

Give your next mix something to learn from.

Label the finished bottle with its recipe, date and nicotine strength. Use the recipe's steep time as a starting point; “Not set” does not mean ready immediately.

For a single-flavour experiment, open that flavour's My tasting tests. Record the percentage, setup, actual steep age and what you taste. Add later tastings to the same test. These records stay private unless you explicitly share a preview as a community note.

What happens to my saved recipes during updates?

Normal app updates are designed to preserve recipes saved in the database. Browser drafts are different: they live on that device and can disappear if browser storage is cleared. Save recipes you want to keep, and keep server backups during beta testing.

If a test mix is unfinished, use My mixes → Abandon to put it aside. It can be restored from Abandoned.
